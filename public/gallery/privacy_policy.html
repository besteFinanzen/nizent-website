2. The short version

Gallery shows and organizes the photos and videos on your phone. Your photos, faces, object labels and photo locations are processed only on your device. We have no server, no user accounts, no advertising and no analytics of our own, and we never receive your photos or any data derived from them.

Three technical connections to third parties exist (see section 5):

  1. Google Play -purchases and subscriptions.
  2. Google ML Kit -the on-device recognition software sends technical diagnostics (no image content) to Google. This only starts after you agree in the app, and you can withdraw your agreement at any time (section 5.2).
  3. OpenFreeMap -map data, only when you open the Map tab.

3. Data processed on your device only

The following data is created and stored in the app's private storage on your device. We have no access to it, it is never transmitted to us, and it is not included in Android cloud backups or device-to-device transfers (backup is disabled for the app):

  • Photo and video metadata (file name, date, size, album, favorites, trash state), read through Android's MediaStore.
  • Face detection results and numerical face descriptors ("embeddings") used to group similar faces, and the names and groupings you assign. Face descriptors can qualify as biometric data. They are computed on your device, stay in the app's private database, are only used to group faces inside the app, and are never transmitted.
  • Object labels (e.g. "dog", "beach") computed on your device by an on-device model.
  • GPS coordinates read from your photos' metadata to place them on the Map tab. They are only used locally; they are not sent to the map provider (see 5.3).
  • Your purchase status (cached locally so that the app works offline).

Our role. The app is software that you run on your own device to look at your own photos. Because we neither receive nor can access this content, we do not decide on the purposes and means of processing it and are therefore not the controller for it. You decide which photos the app may access (including selected photos only on Android 14 and later) and you can delete the derived data at any time:

  • Menu → Settings → Reset face groups deletes all detected faces, descriptors, groupings and person names.
  • Menu → Settings → Reset object search deletes the object-label index.
  • Clearing the app data in Android settings or uninstalling the app deletes everything the app has stored.

Please keep in mind that photos can show other people. If you use the app beyond purely personal or household purposes, you are responsible for complying with data protection law yourself.

4. Permissions

  • Photos and videos (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED, on Android 12 and older READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE): to show and organize your library. On Android 14 and later you may grant access to selected items only.
  • Media location (ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION): to read the GPS position stored in your photos for the Map tab. The app does not request access to your device's current location.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS): to show progress while the on-device index is built. Optional.
  • Internet and network state (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): for map data, Google Play billing and ML Kit diagnostics (section 5).
  • Background work (WAKE_LOCK, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, FOREGROUND_SERVICE): Android's WorkManager uses these to continue the on-device index when the screen is off or after a restart. Nothing is transmitted in the process.
  • Vibration (VIBRATE): used by notifications.
  • Billing (com.android.vending.BILLING): to buy and verify the subscription or the lifetime purchase through Google Play.

You can withdraw permissions at any time in the Android system settings.

Subscriptions and the lifetime purchase are sold and paid for through Google Play. The Google Play app on your device tells our app which products you own (product, validity) so that the app can be unlocked. As the app provider we may additionally see order information in the Google Play Console (e.g. order number, product, date, country, amount, refund or cancellation status). Google processes your data as an independent controller; the data protection information of Google applies (https://policies.google.com/privacy). Legal basis for our part: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of the purchase and licence contract) and Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR (accounting obligations).

5.2 Google ML Kit (face detection and image labeling)

The ML models run on your device. Face detection and image labeling are optional: they only run after you enable on-device AI in the app (Settings) and consent to this notice. Until then the ML Kit software library is not initialised and transmits nothing. Once enabled, it sends technical diagnostic and usage data to Google over HTTPS: device information (manufacturer, model, OS version, build, available ML accelerators), app package name and version, a per-installation identifier, performance metrics, API configuration (e.g. image format and size), event types and error codes. Google states that this identifier is not intended to identify a user or a physical device and that the data is not shared with third parties. No image content, faces, labels or locations are sent. Your IP address is necessarily visible to Google when the data is transmitted. Google offers no supported switch to disable this transmission; you stay in control by not enabling (or disabling) on-device AI. Details: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure.

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and § 25(1) TDDDG) for enabling the feature, and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR for the technical diagnostics described there while the feature is enabled; § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG covers the local storage itself. The diagnostic data is limited to technical information without content. Withdrawal: at any time under Menu → Settings → About on-device AI → Withdraw consent. Afterwards no further scans are started and ML Kit is not initialised again; an already running app process may keep the library active until you close the app. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing up to that point, and you can delete the data already created on your device as described in section 3. Google may process data in the USA on the basis of the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses. For the device access by the library, see section 7.

5.3 OpenFreeMap (map data)

Only when you open the Map tab, the app loads map data (map style, tiles, symbols and fonts) directly from OpenFreeMap (https://openfreemap.org, operated by Hyperknot Software Kft., Hungary, using the Cloudflare content delivery network). This transmits your IP address and request metadata (requested map area, time, app and system information) to these providers. The requested map area reveals the region you are viewing, but not your photos or their coordinates. According to its privacy statement (https://openfreemap.org/privacy/), OpenFreeMap uses no cookies or tracking, does not store IP addresses in its regular access logs, may log IP addresses in error logs for up to 7 days, and may temporarily log IP addresses for up to 30 days during security incidents. Cloudflare may process connection data as described in its own privacy policy.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the map function you requested). If you do not want this connection, do not open the Map tab. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the ODbL.

We do not use any other analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking services. We do not sell data.

Data held by Google and OpenFreeMap is kept according to their respective policies.

7. Access to information on your device (§ 25 TDDDG)

The app stores information on your device (databases, preferences, purchase status) only to the extent necessary to provide the functions you have requested (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). We do not use cookies, advertising IDs or tracking technologies. If -and only after -you enable on-device AI, the ML Kit library mentioned in section 5.2 reads technical device information and keeps a per-installation identifier for the diagnostics described there; this takes place inside the library and serves exclusively the technical operation of the recognition functions you use. Until you enable it, the library is not initialised.
